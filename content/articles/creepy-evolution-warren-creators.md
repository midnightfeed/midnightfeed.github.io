Russ Jones was alone in his studio, leafing through Wally Wood's old EC tear-sheets, when Jim Warren called, furious, demanding a name for the comics magazine they had been calling Project D. Jones happened to be looking at a word balloon over one of Graham Ingels's Old Witch panels. One word in it jumped out. He said it into the phone, and the magazine had its title.

*Creepy* ran for 145 issues under Warren, from 1964 to February 1983. It kept that name, its black-and-white format and its host, Uncle Creepy, the whole way. Almost nothing else stayed put. The people who wrote and drew it turned over so completely that the run reads as a history of who was available to make American horror comics, and on what terms, across two decades.

## The EC reunion, 1964–1967

Jones had pitched Warren, publisher of *Famous Monsters of Filmland*, on horror comics in the manner of EC. The magazine was planned at 64 pages, he later recalled, and cut to 48. Jack Davis drew the first cover. Joe Orlando drew for it and was credited on the masthead with "Story Ideas." Sold on the newsstand like *Mad*, it sat outside the Comics Code that had driven EC out of horror.

Jones fell out with Warren in 1965 and left. Archie Goodwin, already writing most of the stories, became editor, and the artists were largely the generation EC had trained or admired: Reed Crandall, Frank Frazetta, Al Williamson, Angelo Torres, Wally Wood, Johnny Craig, Alex Toth, John Severin, Gray Morrow, Steve Ditko, with Neal Adams and Dan Adkins among the younger hands. Goodwin asked each artist what kind of story he wanted to draw before he wrote it. He adapted public-domain classics too, at first changing their beginnings or endings, then deciding that was presumptuous and keeping closer to the source.

The line grew around him. *Eerie* began as a companion, its first issue a 200-copy ashcan that Goodwin and the letterer Gaspar Saladino put together overnight. *Blazing Combat*, an anti-war war comic, ran four issues. *Creepy* went bimonthly by the end of 1965.

## The lean years, 1967–1970

It didn't hold. Goodwin resigned after #17, cover-dated October 1967, as Warren went through a financial crisis. Pay for writers and artists was cut, most of the leading artists left, and much of each issue was filled with stories from the Goodwin years. Reprints stayed prominent until #32, in April 1970. Bill Parente, Nicola Cuti and Jim Warren himself took turns as editor.

Recovery came from a new title. *Vampirella* launched in 1969 and the company picked up. Frazetta, Crandall and Wood came back, and so did Goodwin, as associate editor on #35 through #39. Issue #32 also carried the first work of an artist named Bill DuBay, who would matter a great deal later.

## Barcelona arrives, 1971–1976

From late 1971 the credits filled with Spanish names. Warren had met Josep Toutain, whose agency, Selecciones Ilustradas, ran a studio in Barcelona. Its artists appeared in *Vampirella* #11 in May 1971 and in *Creepy* by the end of that year; by 1973 they drew most of the covers and stories across the line. Esteban Maroto, Jaime Brocal, Luis García, Fernando Fernández, José González, José Beá, and the cover painters Sanjulián and Enrich among them. A second group from the agency's Valencia studio, including José Ortiz and Luis Bermejo, followed in 1974.

DuBay became editor of the line in late 1972, and *Creepy* went to nine issues a year. His run is the era of the themed issue: two Poe issues (#69 and #70), three Christmas issues, three given to a single artist, a science-fiction issue, and #64, every story spun from its cover painting. Stories began appearing in colour, many by Richard Corben. Doug Moench, Steve Skeates and Budd Lewis were among the writers, and late in the run Toth and Severin returned. Goodwin came back to edit #61 through #64 in 1974, until questions of authority with DuBay, which Warren never settled, ended the stint early.

## Wrightson's two years

Those four issues brought the decade's most celebrated newcomer. Bernie Wrightson had read *Creepy* from the start; his first published drawing ran on a *Creepy* fan page in 1966. He lost interest in the reprint and Spanish years, he said later, and by 1974 he was at DC, worn out on *Swamp Thing*.

In a 1999 interview with *Comic Book Artist* he gave his reasons for moving. He recalled earning $65 a page at DC and being offered $110. Warren returned original art, which, he said, DC then did not. And Warren printed in black and white on a bigger page, so every line would be reproduced, not lost under muddy colour.

His first Warren job, by his own account, was Poe's "The Black Cat," which he adapted himself, in #62, May 1974. "Jenifer," written by Bruce Jones and drawn in grey markers, followed in #63. The frontispieces were his own idea, and the pen work, he said, was a dry run for his *Frankenstein* illustrations. After a couple of years he left, burned out on comics. Asked for one word for his time at Warren, he chose "Happy."

## Louise Jones, and the end

DuBay stepped down as editor after #78. His assistant, Louise Jones, succeeded him, credited as senior editor from the May 1976 issues, and ran the magazine through #116 in March 1980, with DuBay as its most prolific writer alongside Bruce Jones, Bob Toomey and Roger McKenzie. Carmine Infantino, formerly DC's publisher, arrived and pencilled more than fifty stories; Wrightson inked a couple. And a wave of artists from the Philippines joined, Alex Niño, Alfredo Alcala and Rudy Nebres among them, and stayed to the end.

After Jones left, DuBay edited again under the name Will Richardson, through #126. Chris Adames and Timothy Moriarty followed. Reprints came back, many issues devoted to one artist's older work. Warren's last *Creepy*, #145, is dated February 1983, and the company went bankrupt.

The magazine outlived it. Harris Publications put out one more issue, #146, in 1985. In 2007 the rights went to New Comic Company, which partnered with Dark Horse to reprint every issue of *Creepy* and *Eerie*. A new *Creepy* launched in 2009, with Wrightson among its artists.

## Where to start

**The *Creepy Archives*.** Dark Horse's volumes reprint the run in sequence, from 2008 on; the first won an Eisner. Read in order, they show every change above as it happened.

**One artist at a time.** Dark Horse's *Creepy Presents* books gather a single artist's Warren work. Wrightson, Toth, Ditko and Corben have each had one.

**The issue numbers as a map.** #1–#17 for the Goodwin years; #32 for the end of the reprint era; #61–#64 for Goodwin's return and Wrightson's arrival; #69–#70 for Poe; #79–#116 for Louise Jones.

**The full history.** *The Warren Companion*, by David A. Roach and Jon B. Cooke, covers the whole company.

Through all of it, Uncle Creepy never changed. Everyone behind him did.
